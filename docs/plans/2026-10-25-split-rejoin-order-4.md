## Brief

### What will change?

Two suggestions on one half of a rejected Enter's link no longer have to be decided together. A note or a given-back ref on that link's pieces now ends the same in every click order, at every step.

### What could go wrong?

Rejecting an Enter waits on more suggestions. No plugin test covers a top suggestion landing before the lower one that unwraps the element. Where two suggestions edit a link's ref, 80 sets of decisions next lacks must be decided together.

## Teach

Two suggestions on one property stack, the second on top. A stack is accepted from the bottom up and rejected from the top down. Accepting an unwrap also waits for every other suggestion on the element. So when the lower one also unwraps the element, neither could go first. Now the upper one may, because the lower value can never show once the upper one lands.

Rejecting an Enter inside a link joins its two pieces. The joined link keeps the left piece's note if it has one, so a suggested note on either piece could change it by order. Now the link stays split until those notes are decided, and a removed piece adds nothing. The Enter also drops the ref from the right piece. Rejecting it restores the ref only if the left piece still has it after the same decisions, even if a suggestion unwraps it first, and never when that piece follows another Enter that stays.

## Main changes

- `decideKey` in `packages/plitejs/src/authored/tree/layers.ts` takes the changes that hold a pending unwrap of the node. A pending step under an accepted step holds the accepted one back, as on next, unless every pending tag in it belongs to such a change. `decideFormats` in `packages/plitejs/src/authored/tree/resolve.ts` passes the changes of the node's unwrap marks. Rejecting a lower step still waits on a pending upper one, and the fork waits and the split-history exemption stay. The lower suggestion stays in review through its unwrap mark, and no stored shape changes.
- `writeTouches` in `packages/plitejs/src/authored/tree/halves.ts` says whether a write of a key on a half can change what rejecting the cut does: the type or a key the halves compare. `touchingOf` uses it, and `Half.stacked` becomes the set of keys two suggestions write, so the rejected cut's plain wait in `packages/plitejs/src/authored/tree/resolve.ts` fires only for a stacked key `writeTouches` counts. `metadataWritersOf` lists the other pending changes that write a metadata key on a half, and an inline rejected cut holds while any of them is pending, the way it holds for a touching change. The cut join, and `joinKeeps` beside it, take no key from a right half the decision removes.
- `reclaimFrom` in `packages/plitejs/src/authored/tree/cuts.ts` gives a dropped key back to the right half only when the left piece holds it as this run decides its writes, not when a raw mark names it. It gives back nothing from a left piece that follows a split that stands. One rule covers a gone, lifted or held left piece. When the run lifts the left piece of a cut it holds, the piece leaves with the cut's end, so `joinRejectedCuts` applies that reclaim to the right piece right away, to each later piece a removal or lift of the middle passes the cut to, and to the right piece of each later cut still pending. It also lists those held cuts, and the resolver lands their give-back in this run instead of keeping the right piece's history for a join that cannot come.
- A deferred cut's halves in `packages/plitejs/src/authored/tree/resolve.ts` keep undecided only their own split's history and the history of splits not rejected, so another rejected split's give-back lands. A lifted left half holds the cut for no metadata writer in `verdictOf` in `packages/plitejs/src/authored/tree/halves.ts`, since no join follows.
- `Half.stacked` skips a writer an earlier decision accepted or rejected, so a landed suggestion under a pending one no longer makes the rejected cut's plain wait fire. That also removes 6 of the gate's give-back coupled sets, where one writer of the right piece is accepted first and the other also removes the left piece.
- `cutVerdict` in `packages/plitejs/src/authored/tree/resolve.ts` makes a lifted left half wait on another change's pending removal of it instead of holding, when removing the half would give back other dropped keys than lifting it does. A probe in which the left half is removed settles nothing, so that removal decides the verdict.
- The emptied-element wait in `packages/plitejs/src/authored/tree/resolve.ts` counts an inline element left with only empty text as emptied. A link joined at once still holds the empty text that stood between its pieces, so deciding at once now waits on its pending writes, as a single decision on the joined link does.

## Evidence

### Requirements

- [ask] A suggested unwrap of an element between the halves must not change the rejoin by decision order, whether it is pending or accepted in the same call (finding 5 of the task brief).
- [ask] Say what a decided unwrap of one half leaves after the reject (finding 7).
- [law] Deciding one at a time in any admissible order equals deciding the same verdicts at once, and no new coupled set appears (the `authored-decision-order` skill's property).
- [law] Stacked suggestions decide from the bottom up, from the top down or together, and column inserts in one group stack this way (`docs/plans/2026-10-10-authored-values.md` line 268). This iteration narrows it for a lower suggestion that unwraps the element.
- [law] Plate's column width suggestions give one result in every order (`packages/platejs/src/features/layout/lib/BaseColumnPlugin.authored-tree.spec.ts`, the stacked width suggestions block).
- [premise] Once the upper suggestion lands, deciding the lower one cannot undo it: accepting the lower change removes the element with the key, and rejecting it changes nothing a reader sees, so no correction it triggers rewrites the key under the landed value. This is the lead's conclusion, which round 1 sharpened from "the lower value can never show", a reason that holds for every stacked step.

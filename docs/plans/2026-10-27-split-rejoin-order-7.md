# A rejected Enter rejoins the same way in every click order, seventh iteration

Status: superseded by docs/plans/2026-10-27-split-rejoin-order-8.md
Topic: cut-join
Playbook: plan

Pivot from iteration 6, `docs/plans/2026-10-26-split-rejoin-order-6.md`. Round 3 of its plan panel broke the per-editor unit cache that round 2 had rebuilt. A collaboration import and an undo left two editors with different units, so one decision applied on one editor and returned `blocked` on the other. A Backspace beside a card and a letter between two long units paid for the whole unit. A blocked release left the freeing editor unlike its own reload. The cache was round 2's replacement, so the mechanism went back to `architect`. Its arena picked stored waits, where a waiting decision saves the Enter it waits on and no unit outlives a decision. Everything else in iteration 6 stays, and this iteration builds on its build.

## Brief

### What will change?

A rejected Enter inside a link rejoins the same in any click order, as in iteration 6. Now each waiting decision saves the Enter it waits for, so every editor, collaborator and reload agrees.

### What could go wrong?

A waiting decision whose Enter an edit removes is dropped, so its suggestion shows undecided again, even a withdrawn one. Undo after a collaborator applied the link is refused. Older editors may apply decisions early.

## Teach

A suggested Enter inside a link cuts it into pieces, and rejecting the Enter joins them back. Iteration 6 made the Enter the one decision that applies its link. Every other decision on the link is saved and waits, and deciding the Enter applies them all together.

Iteration 6 kept, in each editor, a live map of which paragraphs belong to which link, and updated it on every keystroke. Review found it went stale after a collaborator's change or an undo, so two editors decided differently, and one keystroke could redo work across thousands of paragraphs.

Now a waiting decision saves which Enter it waits for. Every editor reads from the saved document whether a decision waits, so they agree. A keystroke only checks the Enters it touches. The paragraphs around a link are worked out only when someone decides, when an edit leaves decisions without their Enter, and once for all such decisions on a load.

## Main changes

- A live cut of an inline element, pending or decided and waiting, seals a unit of top-level blocks, derived from marks and verdict records. The unit holds every block, in any root, that names a live mark of a change another block of the unit names, whatever its incarnation, while that change has a pending cut, so one change's two incarnations never sit in two units that each wait on the other. A verdict lands on every live mark of its incarnation, so the unit also holds every block with a live mark of an incarnation that is decided and waiting and, while a decision call is routed, of each incarnation the call decides, through that incarnation's own marks only. One decided suggestion that touches two links with pending Enters therefore puts them in one unit, instead of making each Enter wait on the other. On each side it also holds every neighboring block with a live suggestion mark or an Editing mark that waits on a pending change, and the block before any block whose own boundary join or split mark can merge the two, and stops before the first block with none of these. An inline cut's split mark on a link is not a boundary mark. It also holds every root a block in it owns, marked or not. Live means a mark the landing will consume, not a reference the ledger keeps after a landing. A decision changes a block only through a mark on it, on a container that holds it or on the boundary of the block after it, so a block the unit stops before survives every outcome and no decision changes it. A cut's pieces on its two sides never join, the unit needs no block past it, and two cut links one such block apart form two units, unless a join or an Enter connects the second link's paragraph with that block. The lander is the unit's smallest pending cut incarnation that still marks a block of the unit. A unit whose cuts are all decided and waiting takes the smallest pending lander they name, so its pending members block that lander and its decided members land with it.
- `decideOn` in `packages/plitejs/src/authored/authored.ts` works out the units around the call's suggestions from scratch (`unitsAround` in `ledger/units.ts`) and routes the call before it resolves. A member that is not its unit's lander stores its verdict records with the lander's incarnation in their body (`waits`), changes no content and returns its id. A lander whose unit still has another pending member returns `blocked` with those members. A lander whose unit has nothing else pending lands it. The call's one resolve takes the verdict of every member of the unit, stored ones included as call verdicts and hidden from the facts it reads, so the landing is the at-once decision. A decided member that names the lander but that an edit moved into another unit with a pending lander does not land. The landing rewrites its records to name that unit's lander, in the same commit. One that keeps a live mark and that an edit moved out of every unit lands with it. A decision an edit moved to another link therefore applies with that link's Enter, whichever Enter is decided first. The landing also rewrites the records of every decided member it takes without `waits`, in the same commit, so the saved document shows which decisions a landing applied. Withdrawing a member stores its reject the same way. The order check's step helper uses the same routing.
- No unit outlives a call, and `ledger/unit-state.ts` is deleted. A wait index (`ledger/waits.ts`) answers from the stored value alone which decided members wait. A member waits while it keeps a live mark, every verdict record on it names a lander, and one named lander is pending. A named lander is pending while a top-level block holds one of its cut marks and it has no verdict, so typed text of the same change does not keep it pending. The book feeds the index the net change in claim counts of each whole-value sync, roots that leave or join included, applied after every root's sync, and its changed verdict records, and `WaitIndex.ofValue` builds the same index for a load. A keystroke whose net claim counts move no lander or member across zero does no unit work and rechecks no waiting member, inside, between or outside units and in a unit that owns a card. The ledger counts every visit.
- The resolver's call outcome takes the mark's incarnation instead of its change id, at every site that reads it, and probe assignments key by incarnation, so a re-proposed change id never takes an older incarnation's verdict.
- A view of the document's facts hides waiting members' verdicts without copying the verdict records. Stored facts and these effective facts are two nominal types. Admission, load, historic repair, compaction, the shown-value settle in `tree/readiness.ts`, which on a load reads the load's wait index, review and content reads, the document that Editing and Suggesting commands edit, command lowering and a decision's resolve take the effective facts, so a raw read there fails to typecheck. A peer that receives a member's verdict changes no content, and a waiting member keeps the marks it had before its decision and reads as pending.
- A stray is a decided member that keeps live marks while no lander its records name is pending, such as after an Editing delete of the Enter it waits on, or after its Enter is decided as a member once two units merged. A lander that holds a verdict without `waits` while a block still holds its cut mark, such as a verdict a peer stored without the editor's landing, keeps its members from straying: they read as decided, and admission's settle applies them in the same commit, as far as it applies the lander's own verdict, as deciding them at once does. Admission and load run one stray pass at the value they settle, after historic repair and compaction, and repeat until a pass writes nothing. A pass works out the units of all its strays together, once, on the value before the pass, and a load with no stray works out no unit. A stray whose unit still has a pending lander, its own or one its waiting Enters name, takes that lander in its records, rewritten under the same id through admission's record changes, because the public record put refuses a rewrite. In a unit with no pending lander, the pass releases every stray Enter together, and the unit's other strays take the smallest of them as their lander, so the result does not depend on which stray the pass reads first. Admission releases every other stray by dropping its verdict records, on every commit kind and on a load, so the suggestion reads as undecided with the marks it holds then, on the editor that freed it, on its peers and on a reload. A released withdrawal brings its suggestion back for review. A release that a collaborator's landing reaches first stays applied. Dropping replaces settling them, because settle keeps a join, split, wrap or unwrap mark and a rejected insertion whatever the verdict, which would leave a released verdict saved but unapplied. No keystroke runs a whole-value resolve. A decision that an edit moves away from its unit keeps waiting for the lander it names, and that lander's landing moves it to its new unit's lander, as the routing bullet says.
- The history entry of a decision keeps the suggestions, by incarnation, whose verdict records it stored with `waits`, and a merged entry keeps those of every part. A saved history keeps them too. Admission refuses with a typed history conflict an undo of that entry when a verdict record on one of those suggestions now exists without `waits`, because a decision on that suggestion has applied: a landing that took it, or another reviewer's decision after a release dropped it. That holds whatever else the entry changed, such as a call that lands one link and stores a decision on another, or an Editing delete merged into it, and for a decision an edit moved to another link whose Enter applied it. A redo is refused when a verdict record on one of those suggestions exists, because someone decided that suggestion after the undo. The entry names suggestions, not record ids, because a record id names its decision, so another reviewer's decision never reuses one. On an undo, a suggestion whose records are gone or all still wait never conflicts, so the undo removes its own records, or does nothing when a release dropped them. An entry that stored no waiting record never conflicts, so the undo and redo of restoring a saved document, of an Editing delete that moved or dropped a decision, of a content replacement or cleanup, of Accept all and of an accept outside every unit apply. A peer's Editing delete rewrites no record, so undoing a decision after it applies what it can, as on next. Today the history plugin reports any commit that throws as `failed` and keeps its entry. It now catches this error, drops that entry and returns `blocked` with the conflicting change ids, and nothing changes. An undo of a landing gives its members their `waits` back in the same commit, so it applies, unless its call also stored a decision that a landing then applied.
- The order check's unwrap family can add a paragraph after the link's, which any change may remove, type into or join into the link's paragraph, a second cut link after a plain paragraph, and a quote around the link's paragraph, so a unit that misses a neighboring block, drops a decided cut or misses its container fails it. The check also decides each order on one editor that keeps deciding and compares it with a fresh load at every step, before orders that reach the same verdicts and value are deduplicated.
- The review list skips a waiting member, decided and not landed, at a path, a point or a range. It reads waiting from the wait index for the incarnation at the mark, so a scoped read never walks the occurrences of a change it skips. The book's other reads that skip waiting changes, `pendingInOrder`, `idsFrom` and `members`, read it the same way.
- Deciding at once takes iteration 4's results for an unwrap of a piece. An accepted unwrap of one half leaves only that half without the link, and the other half takes back what the split dropped. Text typed between the halves joins the rejoined link, as on next. An element between them keeps them apart, and one the decision unwraps keeps them apart only through text it lifts out, so an unwrapped element the decision also empties lets them rejoin.

### Design

`architect` ran an arena of three runners on the outcome and the round 3 findings: opus, gpt-6-astra and gpt-6.1-sol, each at xhigh, with the brief asking them to attack whether units must stay live between decisions. Opus deleted the cache and stored each waiting decision's Enter. Astra and sol, independently, kept live units on a dynamic connectivity index with directed ownership reach, and both said it cannot meet the bounded-work law: a name whose last claim goes or comes back, a change in which roots a unit owns, and a replacement-edge search can each pay for a whole unit, and their bounds are amortized. An anonymized cross-judge scored opus's 24 and the other two 19 each, and picked opus's as the base. The lead agrees.

The judge found one correctness gap in the base: load did not retarget, so a merged value with a stray read differently on a peer that loads it than on one that imports it. Load now runs the same stray pass. Grafts: release by settle on every commit kind, which deletes the dissolution resolve (astra, sol); the two fact types (astra); a waiting oracle and a unit oracle written apart from the code they check (astra, sol); the order check's continuing editor compared before deduplication (astra, sol); the history conflict, which no runner produced and the review law asks for (lead). Cases the losing candidates handled that the base drops: a decision an edit moves out of its unit applies at once there, and stays waiting here; a freed rejected insertion is removed by the freeing editor in iteration 6, and shows undecided again here. Both are Defaults rows.

### Premises proven

Five probes ran the design's assumptions about existing code on iteration 6's build, through the public editor and a real Yjs pair. All five hold, and two of them change how a step is built.

- A saved decision can carry the Enter it waits on. The decision parser ignores the extra field, a load with and without it gives the same content and review list, and admission accepts a rewrite of the same record under the same id, with or without the collaboration tag. The public record put refuses that rewrite as a conflict, so the stray pass writes it as admission's own record changes.
- A saved decision that no unit holds settles the same on a reload, a tagged import and both Yjs peers. An accept of an insertion applies and drops the mark, and a reject of an insertion leaves it pending with its text. Plan panel round 1 showed settle also keeps join, split, wrap and unwrap marks whatever the verdict, so release drops a freed decision instead of settling it.
- A load hands back the settle's repair as a change: a saved accept of a pending format loads with the mark gone and the landed write saved.
- When two peers rewrite the same decision offline, Yjs keeps the body from the peer with the higher client id, and both peers, a third peer that joins later and a load of the merged value read that body.
- An undo whose commit throws returns `failed` today, reports the error and keeps the entry on the undo stack, so a lasting conflict fails on every press. Nothing in the packages returns the `blocked` result with conflicts that the history outcome type declares. Step 5 adds that path.

### Costs that remain

- A keystroke that leaves decided members stray works out their units once, together, at most the size of those units, to find another pending Enter. That keystroke removes the claims of the Enter they wait on, such as an Editing delete of it.

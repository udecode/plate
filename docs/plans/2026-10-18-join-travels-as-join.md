# A join reaches the undo as a join

Status: done: landed on next; folded into the transactions subject
Playbook: plan

**Pivot.** This iteration finishes the undo part of suggested task t_dd0dd0bd, which `2026-10-17-join-keeps-target.md` carries. That plan lets a reviewer accept a join beside another author's pending text, which the old hold blocked, so Bob's undo after such an accept must land in the joined line instead of bringing the join back. The last iteration, `2026-10-18-rehome-merger-reading.md`, found the merged line by its text inside the peer's change. Its plan panel's round 1 found 11 critical cases across 7 mechanisms. A probe then showed that no reading of the change can be exact. A real join and a retype reach Bob's peer as byte-identical changes, and the undo law needs different answers for them (`architect-4/a4-premise-probes-a1.log`). The fourth arena then picked a design that never reads a merge from values. Its rounds count from zero.

## Brief

### What will change?

Undo an edit you made in two lines a teammate then joined, and your text comes back where it was, even after Accept all on a quote beside pending text. Sync now sends the join itself, not a retype.

### What could go wrong?

If the same update also moves a joined line, or code rewrote both lines whole instead of removing the break, your undo lands by position, as today. A join someone accepted can then come back.

## Teach

When two people edit at once, each editor replays an undo on top of what the other person did since, and both must end on the same document.

When a teammate joins two lines, their editor records only that the break between them went away. Sync then rewrites it as text removed in one line and typed in the other. Your editor can't tell that from retyping, so your undo lands by position. Your restored text can land in the wrong order, or a join someone accepted comes back.

Now the join stays a join. The teammate's editor sends it as a move of the second line's text into the first, and yours reads that move back as a join. Your undo lands where it was. Where the lines meet, texts stay apart unless their formatting matches. Lines your undo puts back between them go after the joined line, and your older undos in those lines still work.

## Design

### The shape

A join is one section of a `RootChange` that deletes the boundary between two sibling lines. On `[heading(ab), paragraph(cd)]`, `mergeNodeChange` writes the two-token form, the heading's close and the paragraph's open, which keeps the two text leaves apart. A local commit shows the four-token form, which also deletes the meeting texts' close and open, after normalization joins the texts. A deletion that also removes text at the end of the first line or the start of the second still holds the join. So does one that removes whole texts or inline elements at either edge, such as a bold word or a link, and one that removes whole lines between the two, as a cross-line delete and two merges in one transaction both write. A deletion that starts or ends inside an inline element holds no join. A section that also inserts plain text where it starts, as selecting across two lines and typing writes, holds the join and the typed text. A line with no text that the join would remove is removed, not joined. Base keeps text restored into it off the other line (`merge-node-contract.spec.ts:469`), and reading it as a join glues that text onto the other line (`r0-core-probes-jproto-a1.log`, `emptied line merged`). Backspace at the start of a line after an empty line is a join in Plate. Plate calls `tx.nodes.merge` there (`OverridePlugin.ts:314-327`). plitejs's merge would remove the empty line above instead (`should-merge-nodes-remove-prev-node.ts:14-16`), but Plate overrides that read, so the empty line stays unless its type's `merge.removeEmpty` rule says to remove it (`OverridePlugin.ts:361-385`). The join removes the second line, which holds text, so it is a join, not a removal. ### The junction rule

A join that also merges the two meeting texts would let a positional rebase spread one text's properties onto the other. Bold restored on `ab` would make the joined `cd` bold too. So `rebaseOverJoins` transforms over each join with the meeting texts kept apart, then settles each junction once both sides apply. Only a junction where a text meets a text settles, by a rule on those two texts alone. Texts with equal properties join. Otherwise an empty one goes, because the other text holds text and is the spacer an inline element beside it needs. Two texts that both hold text stay apart, and of two empty texts the one with no properties stays. No other child of the line is read or rewritten, and a join of two containers, such as two quotes, has no texts that meet, so it never settles. A test holds each settled junction unchanged under the editor's own rule, `canonicalizeInlineChildren` (`content-completion.ts:181`), with the default schema's empty-text policy. The test may import that rule, and `src/core/change/` may not. Round 1's plan ran the whole line through that rule with every element child counted as inline. That put a spacer before a mixed-content section's heading and spacers around two paragraphs when two quotes joined, and the editor refused both results (`r1-components-jproto-a1.log`, `r1-probes-jproto-a1.log`). The plan reviewed in round 2 passed it only the two texts, but without the empty-text policy, so an empty bold text left beside a link stayed and the editor refused the result in both orders (`r2-codex-r2-settle-jproto-a1.log`). `rebaseOverJoins` runs before the transform's separated-ranges return. That return would otherwise rebase a four-token join and a format change on either text by position, which spreads the bold onto `cd` on base (`r0-core-probes-nextbase-a1.log`, `four-token bold-first`).

Whole lines one side inserts between the other's two lines move first. The join merges the line that holds the first line's text with the line that holds the second line's text, and whole lines restored between those two go after the joined line. Bob's undo of a Backspace in a blank line restores such a line (`merge-node-contract.spec.ts:516`). In the seats' round 1 case, Bob deletes from `abX` through `Y` in `[H(abX), P(Y), P(cd)]`, Alice joins what is left, and Bob undoes. The prototype and base both give `[H(abX), P(Ycd)]`, which splits Alice's join, and this plan gives `[H(abXcd), P(Y)]` (`partial-text-restored-break` in `r1-core-jproto-a1.log` and `r1-core-middle-nextbase-a1.log`; through two base peers, `r1-partial-nextbase-a1.log`). With no text before the break, `[p(ab), h(XY), p(cd)]` gives `[p(abcd), h(XY)]` where a positional rebase gives `[p(ab), h(XYcd)]`. The join stays, and the restored line comes back after it. Text restored at the start of the second line's text stays with that text. In `[p(ab), h(XY), p(Zcd)]`, Bob deletes from the start of `XY` through `Z`, Alice joins what is left, and Bob undoes. The plan gives `[p(abZcd), h(XY)]`. Splitting the insert at its first break would part `Z` from `cd`, and a positional rebase would put the heading's `XY` into the paragraph (round 2, Opus).

The returned change keeps every token it does not move, and the transform recognizes the moves it wrote. A token change has no move, so the change deletes the restored lines where they stood and inserts a copy after the joined line. History rebases each older entry over that change (`history-replay.ts:454`), so an older entry inside the deleted lines would do nothing on undo. On the prototype, Bob types `X` in `P(Y)`, removes `P(XY)`, Alice joins, and Bob's second undo is empty, where base removes the `X` (`r2-sol-r2-history-jproto-a1.log`, `r2-sol-r2-history-nextbase-a1.log`). So `joinsOf` reads the whole lines that a join's change deletes between the two lines and inserts, equal and in order, right after the joined line, as one moved run. An edit inside the run maps into the copy by the run's offset, and the change copies the run as that edit leaves it. Two equal restored lines map by position, not by content. `getMove` (`root-change.ts:2216`) and `mergeNestedChangeThroughStructuralRelocation` (`:1622`) already read a move by equal content, and reading this one costs the size of the run.

A prototype in a scratch copy at `c8850414e4` ran 17 core cases, each in both argument orders (`panel-plan-4/r0/proto-join.ts`, `panel-plan-4/r0/join-probes.mjs`). They cover formatting on either text of a four-token join, a chain with an empty middle line, two joins in one change, an empty second line with a bold text restored before it, a block and an inline text at the junction, the same join on both sides, a join inside a quote, both shapes of a restored line break and a two-token join whose second text the other side empties. 16 give the expected tree in both orders (`r0-core-probes-jproto-a1.log`). The 17th, an emptied line merged away, glues on the prototype and on base alike, because that case keeps base's path. Its Yjs test passes on base through the value export (`merge-node-contract.spec.ts:469`). Base's transform gives the expected tree in 6 of the 17 (`r0-core-probes-nextbase-a1.log`). Round 1's seats added the mixed-content section, the quote merge and the restored breaks with text above, which the build's tests take.

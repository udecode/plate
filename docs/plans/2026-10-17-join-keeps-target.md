## Brief

### What will change?

Deleting in Editing removes text and links at once, and an accepted join keeps its target line's type. If a teammate merges two lines and you undo typing or deleting there, the undo lands in place and their merge stays.

### What could go wrong?

If the teammate's same update also moves one of the merged lines, your undo still lands by position, as today. A join accepted into a line you emptied can show its pending insertion twice, which today happens only without other pending text there.

## Teach

In Suggesting mode every edit waits for a reviewer. In Editing mode an edit applies at once. A suggested line join waits too, and once someone accepts it, the joined line takes the type of the line it merges into, such as a heading.

Today, if you delete in Editing the whole line such a join merges into, the editor holds your deletion back so the join still sees text there. The text then shows as if you never deleted it, a review leaves a deleted link behind, and undo after a teammate accepts the join can bring the join back.

Now your deletion happens at once, and an accepted join keeps its line's type no matter what is left in that line. When a teammate merges two lines and you undo text you typed or deleted inside them, the editor undoes it in the merged line, so their merge stays. Any other undo works the way it does today.

## Design

### The rule

An accepted join lands its line with the type and properties of the line it merges into, the nearest line before it that the decision keeps, whatever that line still shows. Nothing new is stored. A rejected split rejoins its pieces into the piece before it while that piece keeps content, and into the piece after it otherwise, as today. The proposed projection holds only accepted joins, so it always merges into the target.

Every route to an empty target now keeps its type. An Editing deletion after the join keeps it (the frame law, T7, E1, E3b, E3d and E5). A suggested deletion accepted first keeps it, in any order. A merge into a line that shows nothing is the one place a plain editor gives the line's type away, and Plite's Backspace there records a line deletion, not a join (`stale-probes-b40-a2.log`). A raw `tx.text.deleteBackward` still records a join there, because raw transforms decide on the stored tree. The two recordings that expected a paragraph now expect the heading. The three input paths that called raw deletes now dispatch commands.

### Answers to the three questions

1. An Editing deletion applies in its own commit, always, so nothing is left to apply when a join goes away.
2. An Editing deletion of a link removes the link with its text through the usual same-commit acceptance. A suggested deletion of a link that a verdict settles removes the link too, through settle's new element rule. A pending line break inside that link moves to the link's other piece, as an Editing removal already does, and when the deleted piece was the end of a once-split link, the line break decides as the resolver decides a pending cut on a piece that does not survive.
3. No hold remains, so a pending fill has nothing to break. A rejected fill leaves the target empty, an accepted fill leaves content, and in both cases the accepted join keeps the heading.

### Undo over a peer's merge

Bob deletes `ab` from a heading in Editing, Alice accepts her pending join of `cd` into it on her peer, and Bob undoes. On next, never flip alone gives `cdab`, `acdb` when Bob deleted only `b`, and `cd` before `see ` in the link case (`next-nf-undo-a1.log`). Alice's merge arrives as an insertion of `cd` into the heading plus a removal of her paragraph. Bob's restored text then ties with `cd` at the same offset, or the value repairs place it after `cd`.

The fix makes the merge travel as a join. Alice's commit already holds it as one section that deletes the boundary between the two lines. The Yjs binding now sends that section as a move of the second line's children into the first, and Bob's peer reads the move back as the join. `rebaseOverJoins` in `join.ts` then rebases Bob's undo over the join's exact shape, so his restored text lands where it stood. Each text keeps its own properties, so a bold `ab` stays apart from a plain `cd`, which the judge found every runner missed. ## Rules

These bind the authored scope, so they live in its subject file once the build folds this plan.

- An accepted join merges its line into the nearest line before it that the decision keeps, and the merged line keeps that line's type and properties, whatever that line still shows.
- A rejected split rejoins its pieces into the piece before it while that piece keeps content, and into the piece after it otherwise. This is current behavior, not law, until the split-order task decides it.
- A deletion Editing records applies in that commit, a pending join beside it included.
- Settling applies a claim as a decision would. Deleted text goes, and an inline element whose deletion is accepted goes once none of its content survives, its pending cuts moving to its other pieces.

The undo rule binds history, collaboration and the authored scope, so it goes in `docs/vision/plite.md`'s history list, after the bullet that says history stores each batch as the commit's inverse (line 607 at `4e332b497c`).

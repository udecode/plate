# Plite: issues and regressions

## Issue corpus shape

- **The fork issue dossier's Status lines are not its claims.** From 2026-05-04 to 2026-05-25 the fork kept one section per reviewed ianstormtaylor/slate issue in `831f5e5da6a37f7d9095c1a81acc6b3de10bd3ba:docs/plite/ledgers/fork-issue-dossier.md` instead of commenting on upstream issues, and allowed `Fixes #...` only once the exact original repro was proven end to end, `Improves` or `Related` otherwise (`831f5e5da6a37f7d9095c1a81acc6b3de10bd3ba:docs/plite/ledgers/fork-issue-dossier.md:12-27`). In 26 of its 216 issue sections the Status line names another claim level than the Decision, such as #4716 (Status fixes-claimed, Decision related), #2500 (Status related, Decision Fixes) and #1770 (Status fixed, Decision related), so the Decision and PR-description text hold what the fork claimed. Limit: counted by script on 2026-10-08, comparing the Status level (fix, improve or other) with the first claim word of the Decision; #4612 and #2051 also read improves-claimed while their Decision names no level and their PR text says Related.

- **The fork's own tracker (udecode/slate #5 to #15).** On 2026-05-10 the fork also reviewed the 11 open issues of its own tracker, udecode/slate, whose numbers are not ianstormtaylor/slate issues and never entered upstream claim counts. On 2026-10-08 a browser row under `apps/plite/tests/plite-browser/donor/examples/` replays ten of them: select-all from a text caret selects the text and paints neither image as selected (#5, `images.test.ts:1010`); copying a clicked image writes `<img>`, the image URL and the editor fragment to `text/html` (#7, `images.test.ts:979`); undo from a new editable-void input removes the inserted void (#8, `editable-voids.test.ts:105`); undo restores the caret after mid-line typing and the range after a partial-selection replacement (#9, #12, `plaintext.test.ts:2397`, `:2431`); a selected mention copies, pastes and cuts (#10, `mentions.test.ts:343`, `:538`); undo restores the mentions document after a select-all replacement (#11, `mentions.test.ts:184`); copied rendered paste-html content pastes back as the editor fragment (#13, `paste-html.test.ts:650`); typing over a selected bold word in the hovering-toolbar example raises no runtime error (#14, `hovering-toolbar.test.ts:122`); and a Bold button in the parent page removes bold inside the iframe editor (#15, `iframe.test.ts:74`). #6, a canceled Chinese IME composition, lost its browser rows with the rendering-strategy-runtime example and keeps the package test 'does not save canceled composition text to history' (`packages/plitejs/test/history/history-contract.ts:2010`). The fork claimed fixes for #5, #7, #8 and #14 and improvements for #9 and #11 (`831f5e5da6a37f7d9095c1a81acc6b3de10bd3ba:docs/plite/ledgers/fork-issue-dossier.md:193-207`, `:6843-7022`, `:7148-7184`). Limits: every row except #15's skips the `mobile` project, #13's also skips WebKit, no row drives a real OS input method for #6, and #9, #11 and #12 each cover one example.

- **The coverage matrix's claim levels and proof bar.** Beside the dossier, the fork kept a PR-facing issue coverage matrix from 2026-05-04 to 2026-07-12. It used the same `Fixes`, `Improves` and `Related` levels plus a fourth, `Not claimed`, for an issue that matched by keyword but that the work deliberately left alone. It also set the proof each kind of issue needed before a claim: a benchmark for a performance issue; browser or device proof matching the claim for a browser, IME, mobile, clipboard, selection or DOM-bridge issue; and docs or examples could fix a docs or support issue only when the corpus ledger classed it as docs, support or examples noise. Each of its 17 dated sync sections, from 2026-05-07 to 2026-07-12, states that it adds no fixed claim; the claims live in its `Fixes` list and its related-issue matrix. Source: `52625e85025313eebd4eeb9be2254249ded58676:docs/plite/ledgers/issue-coverage-matrix.md:17-31`, sync sections `:80-294` and `:503-611`.

- **The coverage matrix's fix claims disagree with each other.** In its final state the matrix lists 36 issues the PR could auto-close (`Fixes` lines) and a related-issue matrix of 199 rows: 36 `Fixes`, 36 `Improves`, 103 `Related` and 24 `Not claimed`. The two lists disagree, so the matrix holds 40 distinct fix claims: the auto-close list leaves out #3964, #3973, #4357 and #5987, which the related matrix marks `Fixes`, and the related matrix has no row for #5605, #5709, #6013 or #6034. Limit: counted by script from the `Fixes` lines and the related matrix's claim column on 2026-10-08; each claim's current proof is on the Slate issues page and in this page's other entries.

- **The gitcrawl sync ledger's statuses are claim levels.** `docs/plite-issues/gitcrawl-v2-sync-ledger.md` holds the manual classification of live Slate issues, and `gitcrawl-live-open-ledger.md` beside it is generated input that takes no manual edits (`gitcrawl-v2-sync-ledger.md:10-12`). Its statuses and its bucket column come from the retired Slate claims mode. `fixes-claimed` means the exact original reproduction was proved and fixed end to end, and only it allows a `Fixes #` line; `improves-claimed` means materially improved with no closure and writes `Improves #`, as for #5811 (`gitcrawl-v2-sync-ledger.md:28`); `cluster-synced` means an architecture owner covers the pressure, never a closure; `issue-reviewed` means routed with no fix claim; `not-claimed` is a deliberate non-claim that names its boundary (docs or example, ecosystem, product, support, release or outside raw Slate); `triage-closed` names its class (duplicate, invalid, stale or maintainer noise); and `needs-repro` and `needs-human` claim nothing. The buckets are `v2-input-runtime`, `v2-dom-selection`, `v2-react-runtime`, `v2-core-engine`, `v2-clipboard-serialization`, `v2-api-dx`, `v2-performance-benchmark`, `needs-repro`, the `skip-*` buckets, `docs-examples`, `ecosystem-boundary` and `already-accounted`. A fixed issue in another editor was provenance for a fresh local invariant, never a Plite closure claim. Source: `52625e85025313eebd4eeb9be2254249ded58676:.agents/rules/issue-harvester/references/slate-claims.md:23-28`, `:38-40`, `:52-68`, `:209-216`, `:273-298`. Limit: the ledger also uses levels the mode never defined, such as `related` (`gitcrawl-v2-sync-ledger.md:29`), `implementation-reviewed` (`:372`) and suffixed forms like `cluster-synced, unchanged` (`:123`).

## Clipboard, serialization and external formats

- **Structural cut and delete keep document shape (#3857, #3801, #3469).** Three reports cut or delete structure and leave the wrong shape: cutting a selected block image copies it but leaves it in place (#3857), cutting across a list leaves a `list-item` where a paragraph belongs (#3801), and copying a whole list drops the wrapping list element (#3469). Plite package tests pin each model path. Cutting a selected block void writes it to the `application/x-editor-fragment` payload, removes it, collapses the caret at the end of the preceding paragraph and requests model-owned caret repair, as one `delete-fragment` command (`packages/plitejs/test/react/dom-coverage-native-bridge-contract.test.ts:1541`). Deleting from the end of a paragraph across a two-item list to the start of the next paragraph leaves one paragraph, `beforeafter`, with no orphan list item (`packages/plitejs/test/clipboard-contract.ts:525`). `state.fragment()` over a whole selected list returns the list inside its `bulleted-list` wrapper (`packages/plitejs/test/clipboard-contract.ts:182`). The fork claimed improvements only (`831f5e5da6a37f7d9095c1a81acc6b3de10bd3ba:docs/plite/ledgers/fork-issue-dossier.md:209-293`). Limits: these are package tests with a fake `DataTransfer`; on 2026-10-08 no browser row under `apps/plite/tests/plite-browser/donor/examples/` cuts an image or a list, and HTML list serialization for other apps is not claimed.

- **Malformed internal fragments fall back to plain text (#5328, #4857).** Slate threw on pasted HTML whose text contained its fragment attribute (#5328) and on foreign HTML pasted after select-all (#4857). Plite reads its internal fragment only as a version 1 envelope and treats anything else as invalid without throwing. A payload with broken URI encoding, text that is not JSON, JSON without a slice, a slice without a version, version 2 or an extra key pastes the `text/plain` fallback ('rejects malformed and non-v1 fragment envelopes', `packages/plitejs/test/dom/clipboard-boundary.ts:2958`); a malformed payload with no fallback changes nothing (`:3000`); and `editor.api.dom.clipboard.readSlice` reports such a payload as `invalid` even when the HTML of the same transfer embeds a valid fragment (`:856`, `:918`). The opening-tag-only attribute lookup that answers #5328's HTML text is on the Slate page (`docs/research/sources/slate/issues-and-regressions.md`, 'Paste and copy fragment policy'). The fork claimed improvements for both (`831f5e5da6a37f7d9095c1a81acc6b3de10bd3ba:docs/plite/ledgers/fork-issue-dossier.md:4023-4084`). Limits: JSDOM package tests with a fake `DataTransfer`; no browser row replays the pasted HTML of #5328 or #4857.

## Selection, focus and DOM bridge

- **A renderer that leaves editable children without DOM is an app error (#5355, #4658).** Slate crashed when arrowing into or out of a table whose renderer adds `colgroup` and `col` and leaves editable descendants unrendered (#5355), and a custom table node let text be typed outside the table (#4658). Plite treats an editable child that a renderer leaves without DOM as an app error unless a DOM coverage boundary claims it: in development builds a guard around each rendered element resolves each child's first text point and logs an 'omitted editable child … without a DOM coverage boundary' error when neither a boundary nor mounted DOM holds it (`packages/plitejs/src/react/components/editable-rendered-element.tsx:39`, `:59-62`, `:76-106`, `:127-163`); the `plate-notes` rendering page gives why the check waits one macrotask. 'renderElement dropping editable children without a boundary reports a dev safety error' and 'BoundaryRange registers before dev safety reports omitted children' pin both sides (`packages/plitejs/test/react/dom-coverage-boundary-contract.tsx:1345`, `:272`). The fork left #5355 unclaimed for this reason and kept #4658 related (`831f5e5da6a37f7d9095c1a81acc6b3de10bd3ba:docs/plite/ledgers/fork-issue-dossier.md:3465-3492`, `:3670-3690`). Limits: the guard only logs through `console.error` and is off in production builds, and no row replays #5355's arrow keys or #4658's typing.

## Performance and scalability

- **History retained memory (#3752).** Slate #3752 reports slate-history keeping old editor or DOM state alive. Plite's `plite-history-retained-memory` benchmark target bounds serialized History size, not retention: it asks whether one 5,000-block History batch keeps one versioned change without its serialized History growing past 2.25 times the input payload (`benchmarks/targets/slate-v2.json:1997-2025`). Its full-document replacement and range-delete lanes throw unless exactly one undo entry serializes as History version 4 and reloads through `History.fromJSON` as one entry; with `PLITE_HISTORY_RETAINED_MEMORY_STRICT=1`, which the target sets, the script also fails without exposed GC or above the 2.25 ratio; heap deltas are recorded but not gated, and the script names #3752 as its issue pressure (`benchmarks/slate-v2/donor/core/current/history-retained-memory.mjs:91-158`, `:212-307`, `:323-368`). The fork claimed an improvement and asked for a leak threshold or detached-retention proof before any fix (`831f5e5da6a37f7d9095c1a81acc6b3de10bd3ba:docs/plite/ledgers/fork-issue-dossier.md:5237-5270`). Limits: no check measures detached DOM or node retention, so #3752 stays unclaimed, as the Slate page says; no run result is pinned here.

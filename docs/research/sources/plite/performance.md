# Plite: performance

## Research verdicts

- The 2026-09-09 editor performance research (iteration 2) ranked full-DOM mount and structural-edit attribution first and decoration-manager setup and update cost second, rejected a blanket piece-tree text store and a universal GPU rich-text renderer, and adopted no production optimization. Source: `docs/editor-audits/reports/editor-performance-research-iteration-2-2026-09-09.md:16-19`; the experiment dossiers are in `docs/plite/research/2026-09-09-editor-performance-iteration-2/experiments.md`. Limit: a preserved summary; the measurements were not rerun when it was preserved on 2026-09-18.

## Huge-document benchmark lanes

- The huge-document browser trace reports selection materialization frames and scroll delta for cold and already-materialized selection (`selectMaterializationScrollDelta`, `materializedSelectMaterializationScrollDelta` in `benchmarks/slate-v2/donor/browser/react/huge-document-browser-trace.mjs:2205-2212`, `:2424-2439`). These metrics were added on 2026-06-12 because CodeMirror's scroll-anchor measure loop showed anchor drift and settle frames are behaviors of their own; a 200-block virtualized smoke then reported 0 frames and 0 px. Source: `docs/plite/research/2026-06-12-huge-doc-architecture/README.md:67-97`. Limit: smoke-sized Chromium run on the pre-rename tree.

## Huge-document measurements

- On 2026-06-12 a one-iteration virtualized browser trace at 100k and 200k blocks (Chromium, pre-rename tree) kept DOM nodes at p95 316 with 0 materialization frames, 0 px scroll delta and type-to-paint about 31-32 ms, while click-to-paint p95 grew to 106.6 ms at 100k and 189.8-315.1 ms at 200k, heap to 179-391 MB and the longest task to 172-392 ms. Capping the example's generated-document cache above 50k blocks cut 200k heap by about 65 MB without changing DOM or typing metrics. The conclusion was that at extreme sizes the cost is model and click-selection work, not DOM virtualization. Source: `docs/plite/research/2026-06-12-huge-doc-architecture/README.md:161-200`. Limit: historical numbers from `.tmp/plite`; rerun `benchmarks/slate-v2/donor/browser/react/huge-document-browser-trace.mjs` before citing one.

- 200k-block click latency came from a content-root owner scan in root mousedown. Before the fix, virtualized click-to-paint p95 was 306.5 ms, of which `root-mousedown.resolve-projected-drag-endpoint` took 270 ms while selection wait was 0.9 ms; after `findContentRootOwners` returned `[]` without scanning for schemas with no content-root specs, and root drag-endpoint resolution reused the resolved start range, click-to-paint p95 was 39.8 ms (2026-06-12, Chromium). The shortcut is still the first check in `findContentRootOwners` (`packages/plitejs/src/react/editable/content-root-owners.ts:141-151`), and the `root-mousedown.*` profiler phases, such as `root-mousedown.resolve-start-range` and `root-mousedown.resolve-projected-drag-endpoint`, remain in `packages/plitejs/src/react/editable/root-interaction-controller.ts:1439-1737`; the browser trace's click metrics split dispatch, mousedown, selection wait and paint wait. Source: `docs/plite/research/2026-06-12-huge-doc-architecture/README.md:202-252`. Limit: a schema with content roots still pays the scan; historical numbers.

- Projecting the view-selection decoration over the whole document made 200k-block select-all-to-paint 251.7 ms. Clipping the June projection to the mounted top-level keys plus the top-level keys of both selection endpoints cut it to 31.4 ms, and leaving the endpoints out lost the off-window focus marker under repeated Shift+ArrowDown (`docs/plite/research/2026-06-12-huge-doc-architecture/README.md:354-389`, 2026-06-12, Chromium). That clipping is gone: today the source builds one decoration per view-selection segment, keyed at the segment start, refreshes only the node keys at the old and new segment starts, and reads per entry (`packages/plitejs/src/react/view-selection-decoration.ts:57-178`). Limit: historical numbers from a removed mechanism; rerun the 200k select-all trace before citing a current cost.

- When model selection is already preferred for the incoming input type, beforeinput skips the pending DOM selection flush (`shouldFlushSelectionChangeBeforeDOMBeforeInput`, `packages/plitejs/src/react/editable/runtime-before-input-events.ts:340-350`), and keydown skips it under a `preserve-model` selection policy (`shouldFlushSelectionChangeAfterKeyDownPolicy`, `packages/plitejs/src/react/editable/runtime-selection-engine.ts:308-316`). At 200k blocks after select-all delete, the beforeinput skip cut physical first-key latency from about 280-296 ms, of which `beforeinput-flush-selection` took about 110 ms, to about 20 ms with no long tasks, and the keydown skip cut virtualized undo-after-type from 288.2 ms to 12.4 ms. Source: `docs/plite/research/2026-06-12-huge-doc-architecture/README.md:1053-1134` (June 2026, Chromium). Limit: the staged lane it also measured no longer exists; historical numbers.

- When a vertical selection extension leaves a rendered line, Plite first tries the cheap adjacent-block visual target (`resolveAdjacentBlockVisualLineTargetPoint` under the profiler label `plain-vertical.resolve-adjacent-visual-target`, `packages/plitejs/src/react/editable/dom-coverage-vertical-selection.ts:836-850`) before the expensive model-line fallback. On 2026-06-12, before that order, a strict 50-step held Shift+ArrowDown from the first block of a 5k-block document failed at step 33 (selected text stuck at 253 characters) with p95 239.7 ms, the model-line target resolution taking about 19 s in total; afterwards the cross-editor run measured p95 15.3 ms (auto) and 15.8 ms (virtualized) for Plite, 15.8 ms for ProseMirror and 16.4 ms for Lexical. Source: `docs/plite/research/2026-06-12-huge-doc-native-selection/README.md:81-93`, `:132-133`. Limit: one-iteration Chromium runs on the pre-rename tree; the June `auto` strategy no longer exists.

## Markdown parsing

- About 80% of Plate's Markdown parse time is conversion, not syntax. The dialect receipt cited on 2026-09-28 measured a full parse of the large legacy cohort at 521.6 ms, of which tokenizing plus the MDAST and registered-tag grammar took 104.4 ms; the rest was mapping dispatch, per-node contexts and admission. Reusing tokens alone could save at most about a fifth of a parse, which is why streaming reuse keeps converted segments (`docs/research/probes/2026-09-28-conversion-boundary/amendment/REPORT.md:57-61`; the report does not name the receipt's path, and the split was not re-measured after the conversion work that followed).

## Document replacement

- A correction after a full document replacement makes representation finalization expensive. On 2026-09-28, with EditorKit and 33 KB of Markdown holding 300 tables (median of 5 runs after 3 warmups, `docs/research/probes/2026-09-28-conversion-boundary/lanes/authored/replace-cost.probe.test.ts`), `value.replace` took 729 ms when the source ended in a paragraph and 963 ms when it ended in a table and `TrailingBlockPlugin` wrote a paragraph; `transaction-finalize-representation` (3 calls) rose from 41 to 223 ms, 140 ms of it `representation-window-apply`, incremental schema validation from 41 to 78 ms and corrections from 72 to 98 ms. The authored runtime adds one `authored-restore` of about 22 ms either way. The owner is Plite core; this was not re-measured after the later per-transaction work (`lanes/authored/REPORT.md:91-103`).

## HTML parsing

- HTML sanitizing is a small share of HTML parsing. On 2026-09-29 with happy-dom and an EditorKit subset (Image, Link, List), rows of a styled paragraph with a link and an image plus a list item with an obfuscated href cost, at 250, 1,000 and 2,000 blocks (48 KB, 194 KB, 392 KB): DOM sanitizer pass 6.9, 8.8 and 9.3 ms; parse5 plus source pass 5.1, 12.2 and 22.1 ms; whole `parseSlice` 120.6, 302.2 and 542.4 ms (medians of five passes). A hostile input of alternating `<p onclick>` and `<script>` siblings, two diagnostics per pair, ran through `parseHtmlAst` in 22, 35, 82 and 195 ms at 5,000, 10,000, 20,000 and 33,000 pairs (up to 1.29 MB), a little faster than the source pass it replaced (`docs/research/probes/2026-09-28-conversion-boundary/lanes/html-safety/REPORT.md:141-169`).

- The parse5 plus LinkeDOM server HTML path passed its frozen four-cohort budgets in the committed run (`docs/plite/research/2026-09-25-document-codec-architecture/html-server-parse5-linkedom-benchmark.json`: darwin arm64, Node v22.22.1, parse5 8.0.1, LinkeDOM 0.18.13 at `fcd88e0`, each cohort in a fresh process). p95 against its budget was 3.64 ms against 25 ms for normal (10,383 B, 362 elements, 20 samples), 89.61 ms against 400 ms for large (356,823 B, 12,002 elements, 10 samples), 1,310.31 ms against 1,500 ms for stress (1,470,823 B, 48,002 elements, 5 samples) and 969.96 ms against 3,000 ms for pathological (3,166,823 B, 96,002 elements, 3 samples). Retained heap was 0.70, 4.40, 0.02 and 3.24 MB and peak RSS delta 11.58, 58.97, 5.67 and 128.93 MB, inside budgets of 24, 128, 256 and 512 MB and 96, 256, 640 and 1,200 MB. The shard's own table (p95 2.41, 52.03, 143.65 and 368.19 ms) comes from a different run with no saved JSON, so cite the JSON. Stress used 87% of its p95 budget, and its p95 (5 samples) exceeds the pathological cohort's (3 samples), so treat the stress figure as noisy. Source: `docs/plite/research/2026-09-25-document-codec-architecture/shards/006-saturation-and-final-synthesis.md:22-32`; the JSON landed in commit `3e0ab4dd7d` on 2026-09-27.

## Per-update cost

- A Plite update now costs in proportion to its change, not the document, for streamed tail edits. Measured on 2026-09-29 (bun 1.3.12, darwin/arm64, shared host at load 5–11) by `docs/research/probes/2026-09-28-conversion-boundary/lanes/p1/per-transaction-cost.ts` on a parsed Markdown preview (120 top-level nodes at 5 KB, 1,179 at 50 KB), as medians of per-run medians over alternating baseline (`a7750ad388`) and candidate pairs: 60 history-skipped tail splices (remove plus insert of the last node) went from 5.3 to 0.9 ms at 5 KB and from 51.2 to 1.3 ms at 50 KB (p90 59.8 to 2.0 ms); the same splices recorded in history went from 16.2 to 1.1 ms at 50 KB; 200 end-of-document keystrokes went from 1.12 to 0.81 ms; and one saved keystroke after 60 skipped splices, which maps its undo entry through all of them, went from 386 to 40 ms. From 5 KB to 50 KB the baseline grew ×9.7 for skipped splices and the candidate ×1.4. Every pair asserted identical values, history depth and undo results. The first splices after a whole-document `value.replace` cost 6.9, 2.2 and 1.6 ms against 86, 17 and 33 ms, because the first resolves the replacement's identity overlay once. These lanes are probes, not registered `benchmarks/targets` entries (`lanes/p1/REPORT.md:78-101`).

- Four O(document) costs in a Plite update were found by profile and removed on 2026-09-29 (`docs/research/probes/2026-09-28-conversion-boundary/lanes/p1/REPORT.md:103-142`). (1) A history-skipped update ran `inverseChanges.apply(after)` in `reduceHistory`, encoding, decoding and deep-freezing the whole document to store a `before` value nothing read (73% of the skipped-splice loop); the skip path no longer computes the inverse, and journal entries store the published `after` value (`packages/plitejs/src/history/history-plugin.ts`, `history-state.ts`). (2) `RootChange.invert` sliced removed content through `DocumentIndex.slice`, which materialized whole-document tokens for any slice outside one text node (71% of recorded splices); it now encodes only the deepest sibling run covering the range, which gives identical tokens because encoding is compositional (11,520 random ranges matched). (3) The snapshot index carried the lazy overlay of every identity a whole-document replacement claimed into every later mapping; a publishing mapping now materializes once and restarts the chain when the overlay passes 256 entries (`MAX_CARRIED_OVERLAY`, `packages/plitejs/src/core/snapshot-index.ts:355`), while spec drafts never rebase because only publication binds node keys. (4) Looking up the path of a node the change deleted materialized the whole index; mappings now carry deleted identities, capped at 256 as a cache, so a known deleted identity resolves to null without a rebuild. Tests that fail on the old code: 'records a history-skipped update without rebuilding the previous document' (`packages/plitejs/test/history/history-contract.ts:297`) and 'restarts lazy identity mapping instead of carrying a wide overlay' (`packages/plitejs/test/document-change.test.ts:1800`).

- Undo replay through history-skipped edits no longer runs value-based transform repairs for separated edits. `RootChange.transformInDocument` returns the positional transform when unchanged content separates the two changes' edited ranges, keeping value-based repairs for edits that touch, overlap or relocate each other, and `sameNodeStructure` short-circuits identical nodes (`packages/plitejs/src/core/change/root-change.ts:1276`, `:1754`). A property check found no divergence across 2,716 random separated pairs of text edits, node inserts and removals, property sets, splits, merges and moves. After all of the 2026-09-29 fixes a CPU profile of the 50 KB skipped-splice loop totaled 90 ms against 3,490 ms on the baseline, and its largest remaining self cost was `Object.freeze` of each new top-level array (`docs/research/probes/2026-09-28-conversion-boundary/lanes/p1/REPORT.md:153-170`).

- Some per-update width cost is a law of Plite's model, not a defect. Published values are immutable plain JSON, so every update allocates a fresh frozen `children` array for each changed ancestor level: O(siblings) at that level, and O(top-level blocks) at the top of a flat document. Copying and freezing a 1,179-element array costs about 111 µs (11 µs at 120). Removing it would need chunked children in the public value, which the simple document model rules out. The laws the 2026-09-29 fixes kept: `NodeKey` stays stable and a known deleted identity resolves to null without a rebuild (`docs/vision/plite.md:606-609`); no history law needs the pre-update document for a skipped update, because replay needs only the change and the value it produced, which the commit publishes; and `DocumentChange.transform` promises pairwise convergence, which positional transforms of separated edits meet exactly (`docs/research/probes/2026-09-28-conversion-boundary/lanes/p1/REPORT.md:215-241`).

- Per-update costs still open after the 2026-09-29 work (`docs/research/probes/2026-09-28-conversion-boundary/lanes/p1/REPORT.md:243-271`; code rechecked 2026-10-08). Replaying history through skipped updates is O(skipped updates × top-level width): `resolveHead` (`packages/plitejs/src/history/history-state.ts:423`) runs two `DocumentChange.apply` calls per journal entry and maps both batch selections, 40 ms for 60 skipped splices at 50 KB and 9 ms at 5 KB, growing with a history-skipped stream; mapping runs of separated entries by position, or carrying `mappedNextBase` forward as the next entry's `nextBase`, would remove it. The Yjs controller rebuilds `previousCommittedValue` with `commit.inverseChanges.apply(committedValue)` on every local commit (`packages/plitejs/src/yjs/core/controller.ts:552`), allocating new top-level arrays although `commit.before` already holds the value. A lazy identity chain that grows past 256 overlay entries through small structural edits pays one materialization, about 2–5 ms at 50 KB, every 256/g edits, where g is the identities each edit claims. `RootChange.between` is still a whole-document token diff, used when an undo target's base differs from the current value, in `DocumentChange.correct` and in overlapping transform repairs.

## Markdown streaming

- In a 2026-09-29 AI-flow benchmark, continuing the previous partial parse and publishing only the changed tail cut cumulative preview work at every size. `docs/research/probes/2026-09-28-conversion-boundary/lanes/s4/ai-flow-benchmark.test.ts` (Apple M5 Max, Bun 1.3.12, load about 3) sent 64-character chunks every 10 ms through `AIChatPlugin.setPreview` and a BaseEditorKit preview editor, publishing the first chunk at once and then the latest prefix every 32 ms, against the plugin at `a7750ad388` publishing by `value.replace`. Cumulative preview work went from 1.41–1.61 s to 196–213 ms (rich 10 KB), 37.5–39.0 s to 1.33–1.45 s (rich 50 KB), 2.77–2.88 s to 0.32–0.33 s (CJK 10 KB) and 70.8–75.3 s to 2.40–2.53 s (CJK 50 KB); `setPreview` p95 at 50 KB fell from 74–130 ms to 3.0–4.3 ms, and 95–99% of earlier blocks kept their objects. Every preview and final was asserted identical across arms, and the strict final costs the same in both. Block identity starts at the second partial preview, because the first parse has no hint (`lanes/s4/REPORT.md:69-92`). Limit: AI previews have since moved to rendering the parse as an `EditorStatic` document (`apps/www/src/registry/components/editor/ai-menu.tsx:142`), so the publication half of these numbers is historical.

- The adopted Markdown streaming path passed its Chromium acceptance matrix: S5 verdict-2, snapshot 2026-09-29T11:59:28Z, unmangled production webpack builds of both arms, Apple M5 Max, chromium-headless-shell 149, load at or below 7.9, with the final tree's continued-parse path byte-identical to the snapshot. Against a baseline that dropped `previous` and published every preview with `value.replace`, parse-plus-transaction work per pair fell 84–97% for static previews, 77–94% for the editable preview, 82–96% for AI with identical prefixes and 66–84% for live AI arrivals; strict-final p95 fell in every cell (for example static CJK 50 KB from 844 to 506 ms and editable CJK 50 KB from 376 to 32 ms), final text was identical across arms, and no stream hit its cap. At that snapshot React render and commit were the largest remaining cost of static and AI previews, 24–51 s at 50 KB in both arms, because the static renderer rebuilt its view on every commit and re-rendered every block; `PlateStatic` now keeps unchanged blocks memoized across commits of one runtime owner (`packages/platejs/src/static/components/PlateStatic.tsx:465-476`). The matrix runs from `apps/www/tests/browser/markdown-streaming-contract.spec.ts` with `S5_BENCH=1` (`docs/research/probes/2026-09-28-conversion-boundary/lanes/s5/REPORT.md:15-65`).

- On the final tree of 2026-09-29, continuing the previous partial parse made preview parsing of a growing Markdown source nearly free (`docs/research/probes/2026-09-28-conversion-boundary/lanes/formats/continued-parse-current.json`, headless, 64-character chunks, summarized in `lanes/s5/verdict-2/SOURCE-DIFF.md:15-28`): cumulative preview parses went from 887 to 63 ms (rich 10 KB), 1,821 to 80 ms (CJK 10 KB) and 33.4 to 0.39 s (rich 50 KB), the final strict parse from 10.2–65.8 ms to 0.2–0.5 ms, and 97–99% of blocks were reused; every strict final equaled a fresh strict parse. A source with a link reference or footnote definition reuses nothing and parses whole on every call (938 to 917 ms at 10 KB), as `parseSlice`'s JSDoc states (`packages/platejs/src/markdown/lib/MarkdownPlugin.ts:73-82`).

- **Before reuse, publication and fitting cost more than parsing a growing Markdown stream.** On 2026-09-28 (Bun 1.3.12, `createTestEditor`, 64-byte ASCII chunks of plain paragraphs and headings, cumulative milliseconds per stream, median of five alternating pairs after one warmup) a 10,000-byte stream cost 520.6 ms in full `parseSlice`, 627.4 ms in a separate `fitDocumentWithReport` plus `assertDocument`, and 1,221.9 ms publishing raw nodes through `value.replace`, which fits again, so the stages must not be summed; one exploratory 50,000-byte pair cost 10,825.1, 14,224.2 and 24,560.9 ms. A restricted reuse prototype cut the 10 KB parse to 80.7 ms (full totals ranged 485.5-603.5 ms, prototype 71.8-91.4 ms), while fit and publication moved by less than the predeclared 20 % and 5 ms rule. Rich Markdown, which fell back to the full parser, showed no win (10 KB parse 1,207.6 against 1,040.7 ms, one pair, no warmup). Raw pairs: `docs/research/probes/2026-09-28-conversion-boundary/streaming/benchmark.json`. Source: `docs/research/probes/2026-09-28-conversion-boundary/streaming/REPORT.md:24-48`. Limit: measured before the `previous` continuation landed; React, DOM and paint were not timed.

- **Full reparsing of a growing stream is quadratic in bytes.** Parsing every 64-byte prefix in full consumed 793,744 cumulative source bytes for a 10,000-byte stream and 19,593,744 for 50,000 bytes; the restricted prototype parsed 21,522 and 107,933. Even admitted prose was not tail-only work, because each update still checked `source.startsWith(previous.source)` and copied the output array. Source: `docs/research/probes/2026-09-28-conversion-boundary/streaming/REPORT.md:69-72`.

- **Publication keeps model identity even when the parser reuses nothing.** Replacing an editor value with freshly parsed nodes kept Plite model object identity and node keys at 9,071 of 9,071 content-equal top-level positions in a 10,000-byte stream and 225,439 of 225,439 at 50,000 bytes, although the full parser reused zero nodes, because the root fitter reconciles children by equality (`reconcileChildrenStep`, now `packages/plitejs/src/core/change/root-change.ts:3287`). Parser allocation savings therefore do not predict fewer remounts. The counts are aggregate position observations over successive updates in headless test-kit editors, not distinct nodes, and do not cover React reconciliation or reordered trees. Source: `docs/research/probes/2026-09-28-conversion-boundary/streaming/REPORT.md:74-84`.

## Measured gaps from the September 2026 iteration

- Full-DOM mount scales worse in Plite and Plate than in ProseKit. On 2026-09-09, 10,000-paragraph full-DOM mount p95 was 528.3 ms for Plite, 1,018.5 ms for Plate and 54.1 ms for ProseKit, against 38.5, 60.4 and 16.3 ms at 100 paragraphs; the clock ends after two animation-frame opportunities, not at first paint. Production profiles then counted 30,008 selector stores and subscriptions, 160,073 layout-effect hooks and 40,003 DOM elements for Plite's 10,000 paragraphs, and 10,014 selector stores and 20,003 elements for Plate. React Compiler settings did not explain the gap (10,000-paragraph Plite p95 524.7 ms off, 518.8 ms with Compiler). No intervention met the 20% and 4 ms adoption gate: the best Plite cut gained 14.6% and the combined Plate cut 15.0% (callback-ref rebinding, an inlined text component, a shared hydration signal, a removed plugin-context spread), and deleting the text subscription broke empty-text leaf repair. The gap remains open. Source: `docs/plite/research/2026-09-09-editor-performance-iteration-2/experiments.md:38-48`; outcomes in `docs/plans/2026-09-10-editor-performance-execution.md:51`, `:78-85`, `:124`, `:130`, `docs/plans/2026-09-10-editor-performance-follow-through.md:275`. The raw receipts sit in an ignored run directory that a fresh checkout lacks; rerun before citing a number.

- Structural edits are far slower than in legacy Slate. On 2026-09-09 at 1,000 blocks, moving a 32-block window took 140.83 ms p95 in Plite against 1.18 ms in Slate (`ianstormtaylor/slate@945a484df2497e4c448b33f417b0de2a49840032`), and removing it 119.28 against 1.10 ms, in 51-sample programmatic batches whose final states matched. At 10,000 blocks, split p95 was 95.7 ms for Plite, 139.9 ms for Plate and 14.4 ms for ProseKit; bold-then-type 105.6, 75.7 and 51.0 ms; and a ten-character burst 225.1, 107.0 and 84.7 ms, one burst rather than ten keystroke samples. Expanded deletion, mixed fragment insertion and wrap or unwrap produced different final states from Slate, so they have no speed rank. Index, fit, scalar JSON and token-encoding interventions all missed the 20% gate, so the gap remains. Source: `docs/plite/research/2026-09-09-editor-performance-iteration-2/experiments.md:50-62`; outcome `docs/plans/2026-09-10-editor-performance-execution.md:52`, `:99`; receipts only in ignored artifacts.

- On 2026-09-09 code highlighting read the changed block's text and reparsed it before reusing token identities; the line-offset scan in the conversion helper is not on the typing path. The September 2026 execution kept the exact grammar and rejected both input-ownership alternatives: forcing the model-input callback raised complete syntax p95 to 949.0 ms, and disabling the native insertion decision reached 573.4 ms, short of the 20% gate. The 10,000-line code route still measured 611.9 ms input, 627.0 ms syntax and 380.6 ms mount p95, with about 57 ms per burst in Lowlight and 106 ms in the decoration refresh. Do not drop highlighting above a size threshold to hide the cost. Source: `docs/plite/research/2026-09-09-editor-performance-iteration-2/experiments.md:64-70`; outcome `docs/plans/2026-09-10-editor-performance-execution.md:53`, `:111`, `:118`.

- Large-table selection stays above its 100 ms budget. Sharing the row and table selection booleans through the existing table and row components cut a complete 40x40 selection p95, which includes selected-cell paint and the Merge toolbar, from 423.9 to 319.8 ms (24.6%), and that change was kept. A CPU profile put the remainder in React before-mutation commits, Radix `getAnimationName`, `cloneValue` and 99,690 element-selected checks, not table topology. Follow-up packets kept one toolbar instance across transitions (2.8-9.4%, not material), rendered expanded controls immediately and deferred them with `useDeferredValue`; the last two failed the first-paint guard. The 150 ms expanded-toolbar timer makes a 100 ms complete endpoint impossible by construction. Source: `docs/plite/research/2026-09-09-editor-performance-iteration-2/experiments.md:72-78`; outcomes `docs/plans/2026-09-10-editor-performance-execution.md:54`, `:120`, `:142`, `docs/plans/2026-09-10-editor-performance-follow-through.md:249-291`.

- Code-drawing rendering follows preview and export demand: an inactive code-only edit makes zero renderer calls, and activating preview after a hidden edit fell from 530.3 to 28.0 ms p95 for Mermaid, 523.3 to 23.0 for Graphviz, 522.6 to 23.1 for Flowchart and 789.4 to 272.8 for remote PlantUML. Visible export demand still prewarms the image, because the Export control needs a rendered image even in code view; a bare `view === 'code'` early return would have broken export. Started adapter work finishes and stale results are ignored; there is no network abort. Owners: `apps/www/src/registry/components/editor/code-drawing.tsx` and `packages/platejs/src/code-drawing/lib/renderers.ts`. Source: `docs/plite/research/2026-09-09-editor-performance-iteration-2/experiments.md:80-86`; outcome `docs/plans/2026-09-10-editor-performance-execution.md:55`, `:114`.

- Decoration-source setup got cheaper without removing the manager, whose fewer subscriptions and transient renderer reads are worth keeping. At 10,000 nodes and 32 sources the manager's mount p95 was 264.94 ms against 144.80 ms for a per-node baseline, and its update 2.01 against 0.82 ms. The kept fix admits compiled immutable inputs to the existing `WeakMap` fast path only after observed reuse and full freezing (`createPliteDecorationManager`, `packages/plitejs/src/react/decoration-source.ts:564`): pathological mount p95 went from 155.09 to 87.47 ms and update from 1.085 to 0.825 ms, with 43 manager contracts and a 10,000-node, 32-source native browser run passing. These are manager operations, not full browser mount latency. Source: `docs/plite/research/2026-09-09-editor-performance-iteration-2/experiments.md:128-132`; outcome `docs/plans/2026-09-10-editor-performance-execution.md:58`, `:74`.

## Stopped and deferred directions

- Several feature paths do whole-input work that nobody has measured on a real route (read 2026-09-09): AI generation preview rebuilds the assistant text and deserializes the growing Markdown (`packages/platejs/src/ai/react/useAIChat.ts`); `packages/platejs/src/excalidraw/react/useExcalidrawSync.ts` serializes the whole scene with `serializeAsJSON` on callbacks before comparing strings (still so on 2026-10-08, `:52`, `:82`); the copied emoji search in `apps/www/src/registry/lib/emoji-data.ts`, which replaced the package search on 2026-10-08, still scans every entry per query and sorts the matches before limiting them; `packages/platejs/src/features/find/lib/BaseFindPlugin.ts` rescans on document commits and publishes every match, where earlier scan-only fixes failed. Commit-to-consumer fanout needs a demonstrated unrelated subscriber before a field-specific publication change. Measure each actual route before changing it. Source: `docs/plite/research/2026-09-09-editor-performance-iteration-2/experiments.md:96-102`.

- Stopped directions from the 2026-09-09 iteration: no facet fast path, because no production facet read is hot; no blanket piece-tree text storage, because a disposable VS Code piece-tree probe at 10,000 lines took 0.141 ms against 4.204 ms for strings on scattered edit-only pairs but 8.798 against 4.555 ms once each pair read the full string back; and no universal GPU text renderer, because rich text adds native selection, accessibility, IME, print and React node views that no equal-contract measurement has priced. Deferred until measured: Oxc transforms (build cost only), consumer bundle cuts with equal configured features, retained-heap paths after history truncation and view teardown, and repeated same-revision geometry reads after the DOM scheduler. Source: `docs/plite/research/2026-09-09-editor-performance-iteration-2/experiments.md:104-112`, `rejected-ledger.tsv:2-4`.

## React Compiler

- React Compiler is not a broad speedup for the editor. In a September 2026 four-arm packet (React 19.2.8, Compiler 1.0.0, Babel 8, production bundles, 408 attempts at 100, 1,000 and 10,000 paragraphs), 10,000-paragraph split p95 fell from 165.0 to 124.5 ms in Plite and from 247.3 to 188.7 ms in Plate with compilation on, while mount p95 barely moved (803.5 to 798.2 ms Plite, 1,532.2 to 1,558.0 ms Plate) and other operations were mixed; the clock ends at frame opportunities, not verified paint. Keep Compiler where its output is correct and exercised, and do not chase transformation coverage as a performance target. Source: `docs/plite/research/2026-09-09-editor-performance-iteration-2/react-compiler.md:1-7`; the 48 per-row deltas sit in an ignored run directory a fresh checkout lacks.

- Compiler diagnostics depend on the Babel pairing. A source probe of 1,547 non-test modules from Plite, Plate, the copied registry and the www shell recorded 1,155 CompileSuccess and 322 CompileError events under Babel 8, 262 of them an assignment-pattern lowering limitation; the same sources under Babel 7.29.0 gave 1,291 successes, 68 diagnostics and no assignment-pattern errors. A CompileError means a function was skipped or diagnosed, not a failed build, and an event count is not a share of components. The Babel 7 remainder flagged ColorInput building a deferred click handler through `Children.map` and CommandRoot holding a mutable imperative store with delayed callbacks; neither proves a bug. A passing `tooling/scripts/check-react-compiler-contract.mjs` does not accept the diagnosed functions. Source: `docs/plite/research/2026-09-09-editor-performance-iteration-2/react-compiler.md:9-27`, source hashes as of 2026-09-09.

- `useGenericSelector` (`packages/plitejs/src/react/hooks/use-generic-selector.tsx`) returns a selected value while a version snapshot notifies React, and a separate committed record holds the latest selector, equality function, selected state and callback error; layout commit rejects a stale render snapshot. Its `'use no memo'` directive (line 114) protects reads through that mutable record, so deleting it to raise Compiler coverage can produce stale derived values. The stronger alternative is one canonical immutable selected snapshot consumed through a proven concurrent selector adapter, keeping inferred inline callbacks, error propagation, changing equality, render aborts, optional stores and exact view routing; it waits for allocation or fanout evidence, and earlier narrower selector attempts failed. Source: `docs/plite/research/2026-09-09-editor-performance-iteration-2/react-compiler.md:48-54`, `experiments.md:88-94`.

## Long-task attribution

- The huge-document browser trace splits long-task time into attributed and unattributed duration. Each phase trace gets a claim width: `none` (no long-task time), `unattributed` (no attribution duration), `partial` or `attributed` (`benchmarks/slate-v2/donor/browser/react/huge-document-browser-trace.mjs:960-995`). Attribution here means the browser's `TaskAttributionTiming` entries, which name a container, not Plite code. Lanes report p95 total, attributed and unattributed duration (:2370-2386), and the `METRIC ..._long_task_unattributed_p95_ms` output is at :3193. In the 2026-06-12 200k-block staged and virtualized run, the post-delete phases measured as follows. Staged: `typeAfterDelete` 19.9 ms, `undoType` 20.4 ms, `undoDelete` 46.5 ms. Virtualized: 19.9 ms, 21.1 ms and 54.7 ms. Both `typeAfterDelete` phases had `longTaskClaim=none` and zero attributed and unattributed time. The ordinary click, select and type lanes reported a global long-task p95 of 835 ms total, 0 ms attributed and 835 ms unattributed. That number warns about browser-owned work and does not prove Plite runtime cost. The run's raw trace output is not committed, so these numbers are unpinned, and the staged lane no longer exists. Source: `docs/plite/research/2026-06-12-browser-longtask-selection-oracles/README.md:36-52`, `shards/001-browser-longtask-selection-oracles.md:22-29`.

## Test and development code in production bundles

- **Two `NODE_ENV` checks escape bundler replacement.** `packages/plitejs/src/react/editable/editing-kernel.ts:554-556` and `packages/plitejs/src/react/components/editable-rendered-element.tsx:30-36` read `NODE_ENV` through `(globalThis as ...).process`, and a bundler `define` replaces `process.env.NODE_ENV` only as that exact expression, so both checks stay in production bundles and run there. They disagree on a page with no global `process`: `isPliteReactDevelopmentEnvironment` needs a defined `NODE_ENV` and reports production, while `shouldAssertEditableKernelTransitions` compares `undefined !== 'production'` and stays on, so an illegal Editable kernel transition still throws (`editing-kernel.ts:557-566`). Evidence: `docs/plite/research/2026-10-02-agentic-e2e-testing/read-log.tsv:406`, `:413` (source read at cf15725603); code reread 2026-10-08. A minified esbuild 0.28.0 production build of the plaintext `Editable` (`process.env.NODE_ENV` defined as `"production"`) kept the `editing-kernel.ts` check (one hit, measured 2026-10-02 with unpinned scratch scripts; `docs/plite/research/2026-10-02-agentic-e2e-testing/shards/007-page-handle-audit.md:120`, `:148`). Limit: the `editable-rendered-element.tsx` check was not counted in a bundle, and a bundler that defines a global `process` changes the outcome.

- **What the always-attached handle cost.** Before `installBrowserHandle()`, every `Editable` attached the page handle. In a minimal production `Editable` bundle (esbuild 0.28.0 on the `plitejs` dist of 2026-10-02 01:33), the handle cost 10,525 B minified, 2,982 B gzipped and 1,725-2,012 B brotli-compressed, 0.78% of the gzipped size. A read-only handle would have cost at most 4,990 B minified and about 1,472-1,500 B gzipped. Three `__pliteBrowserHandle` references survived minification. Evidence: `docs/plite/research/2026-10-02-agentic-e2e-testing/read-log.tsv:466`; the measurement scripts ran from session scratch and are unpinned. Limit: `tooling/scripts/measure-browser-handle.mjs` exists, but no check runs it, so the claim that production `plitejs/react` drops the handle has no automated gate (`docs/plans/2026-10-02-proof-device-lane.md`, Open work).

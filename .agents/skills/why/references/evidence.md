# Evidence for rationale

Start with the concrete source change and its linked decision. Broaden toward the question: product intent usually lives in issues and design documents; a threshold may need a measurement or incident; a defensive branch may need an error event.

Record event time, author, scope, and the source location. Publication time and search order do not rank authority. A newer unrelated comment does not supersede an earlier scoped decision.

- Direct rationale: an attributable source explicitly connects the choice to its reason.
- Supported inference: several independent observations fit the explanation, without an explicit decision. State the inference and what could disprove it.
- Contradiction: sources disagree; show the relevant scope and dates rather than silently selecting a story.
- Unknown: available evidence does not answer the question. List the consequential search/access gap.

A missing result is not evidence that a decision was never documented. Logs and analytics can prove observed behavior or distributions, not the author's motivation. Do not expose private event payloads or query broad personal history.

For an incident, keep trigger, impact, mechanism, mitigation, durable repair, and validation distinct. Avoid attributing causation from a graph's timing alone.
